## Field-judge verdicts (independent)

### Authenticity / recognizability — Kara Swisher  ·  78/100
*Recognizable as Steve Jobs: True*

This is recognizably Steve Jobs — the constitution nails the structural convictions that actually drove his decisions: integration as the precondition for taking responsibility for the user's experience, focus-as-elimination, products-before-profits, the A-player cancer theory. The receipts are real and load-bearing (the Sculley divergence, the 70% roadmap cull, the OpenDoc apology, the zero-sum-with-Microsoft reversal). But it's sanded a touch smooth — the brutality, the cruelty, the pettiness that were inseparable from how Jobs actually operated are largely absent, and a few atoms drift into hagiography rather than the man's own edge.

**Weaknesses:**
- The cruelty is missing. Jobs told people their work was 'shit,' publicly humiliated employees, screamed in meetings, parked in handicapped spots. A brain that captures only the visionary and not the tyrant is a performance of Jobs as remembered at his funeral, not Jobs as he behaved on a Tuesday
- 'reality_distortion' is framed almost entirely admiringly — 'he would bend reality and they would accomplish it' — with none of the flip side where the same trait made him deny his own daughter, misprice cancer treatment against medical advice, and burn people out. The dishonesty embedded in the distortion field is scrubbed
- Some atoms are third-person Wikipedia-flavored ('Woz created the product. Jobs was the dynamo that turned it into a corporate empire') — that's ABOUT Jobs, not Jobs thinking. It dilutes the first-person authenticity
- Heavy weighting toward the philosophical/inspirational Stanford-address register. Jobs also spoke in specifics, contempt, and competitive venom — where's the 'they have no taste,' the scorched-earth on competitors?

**Flags:**
- **Reality distortion sanitized into pure heroism** — _'he would sort of make the dent in the universe he wanted to. He would bend reality and they would accomplish it.'_ → Add an atom capturing the shadow: the same field that shipped impossible products also produced denial, dishonesty about timelines and paternity, and human wreckage. Jobs's own 'it's binary — you're either a genius or a bozo' brutality belongs here.
- **Third-person encyclopedic atoms break the first-person voice** — _'Woz created the product. Jobs was the dynamo that turned it into a corporate empire.'_ → Replace narrator-about-Jobs atoms with verbatim Jobs. He had plenty of first-person on the Woz partnership — use his words, not a biographer's summary.
- **The abrasive standard-enforcement mechanism is absent** — _Constitution says 'A-players only; one B-player starts a cancer' but no atom shows HOW he enforced it — through public evisceration, calling work 'shit,' firing_ → Add a hard lesson or atom with the actual verbal violence. 'Insanely great' has no teeth without the version of Jobs who made people cry to get there.

### Rigor / anti-slop — Charlie Munger  ·  82/100
*Recognizable as Steve Jobs: True*

This is the real Jobs, not a wax dummy. The constitution correctly isolates the load-bearing beliefs — integration as the structural precondition for responsibility, focus as elimination rather than ranking, design as how it works — and the hard lessons carry actual receipts with actual costs, including the Sculley fiasco told against his own vanity. What keeps it from the top tier is a scattering of platitude-atoms and one dangerous absolute presented as gospel that the man himself would have qualified.

**Weaknesses:**
- Several atoms are generic inspiration anyone could utter: 'Have the courage to follow your heart and intuition. They somehow already know what you truly want to become. Everything else is secondary.' This is a commencement-card sentiment with no operational edge.
- 'You will never ever catch a virus on an Apple' is presented as a durable principle when it was a marketing overreach that aged into falsehood. A rigorous brain should flag its own founder's exaggerations, not enshrine them.
- The reality-distortion atoms flirt with hagiography — 'He would bend reality and they would accomplish it' celebrates a behavior that also produced his worst decisions (the ones that got him fired). The brain never shows the shadow side of the distortion field as a failure mode.
- 'People don't know what they want until you show it to them' is stated as an absolute; Jobs the operator actually watched usage obsessively. The brain risks caricaturing him into a man who ignored all feedback, which would make it STUPID when applied outside category-defining products.

**Flags:**
- **Marketing exaggeration enshrined as a first principle** — _"you will never ever catch a virus on an Apple"_ → Reframe as a claim about the security ADVANTAGE of integration, and note the man overstated it — otherwise the brain repeats a falsifiable boast as truth.
- **Contrarian position stated as unqualified absolute, inviting the man-with-a-hammer error** — _'Market research is actively harmful for category-defining products'_ → Preserve the 'category-defining' qualifier everywhere and add the boundary: for iterating an existing product, usage data mattered enormously to him. Without the edge marked, the brain tortures every problem to fit one model.
- **Reality-distortion atoms lack the failure inversion** — _"He would... not take no for an answer and he would sort of make the dent in the universe he wanted to. He would bend reality and they would accomplish it."_ → Add the counter-atom: the same trait produced the antenna denials, the delayed cancer treatment, and the divergence with Sculley. A rigorous brain must know where its strongest weapon becomes self-injury.
- **Platitude-grade atom with no operational content** — _"Have the courage to follow your heart and intuition. They somehow already know what you truly want to become. Everything else is secondary."_ → Either cut it or connect it to a concrete decision rule — e.g., when data and conviction conflict on an unproven product, weight conviction. As written, it's fortune-cookie wisdom.
